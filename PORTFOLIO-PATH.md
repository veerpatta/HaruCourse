## Project 1 · Research-led everyday service (m05–m11)

### Local booking

Explore how a small service handles finding availability, preparing for an appointment and changing a booking. The owner needs manageable coordination effort; do not assume a new app is the answer.

People: Adults who recently booked a local class, repair or personal service, plus the person coordinating it.

Access: Choose a service where you can invite an organizer and recent customers with permission. Seek different experiences, including a changed or failed booking; label the sample exploratory.

Constraints: Design for mobile use and plain language. Exclude payments, medical records and real booking transactions from the prototype. Staff capacity is a question to investigate.

- Where does uncertainty arise in the current process?
- What information is needed before committing?
- How are changes handled and who does the extra work?

### Community participation

Investigate joining and preparing for a neighborhood activity. Organizers need clear communication without taking on unmanageable administration.

People: Adult newcomers, returning attendees and an organizer of an accessible local activity.

Access: Use a group you can approach legitimately. Ask separately for research participation; joining the activity is not consent to research.

Constraints: Do not expose member lists or exact private addresses. Consider language, device and connectivity needs; prototype with invented logistical details labelled as such.

- What makes a newcomer hesitate or abandon joining?
- How do people find preparation and access information?
- What happens when plans change?

## Project 2 · Responsive web product (m12–m15)

### Small-seller ordering

Explore how a small seller communicates product availability and receives order requests across phone and desktop. The business needs accurate requests and manageable follow-up.

People: An independent seller and adults who have recently tried ordering from a small business.

Access: Recruit through an owner who agrees to the study; invite customers independently without receiving a private customer list.

Constraints: Use mock products and no real checkout, payment or customer records. Address unavailable items and request confirmation. Keep implementation to a bounded local web prototype.

- Which product details affect a decision?
- Where do customers and sellers misunderstand order status?
- What changes between narrow and wide screens?

### Information discovery

Explore finding a suitable class, public activity or learning resource among many options. A provider needs discoverable information that is practical to maintain.

People: Adults recently comparing such options and someone responsible for maintaining the information.

Access: Choose a topic with reachable searchers and a willing information owner. Use publicly accessible, attributable source material.

Constraints: Avoid medical/legal advice directories. Record source freshness; use a small sample catalog and design empty, ambiguous and no-result states.

- Which criteria distinguish a suitable option?
- What labels match the searcher’s language?
- How do people judge whether information is current?

- m13: document the component system, rejected choices and behavior at different widths.
- m14: save delivery criteria, QA evidence and revisions.

## Project 3 · Complex workflow (m16–m18)

### Small-team request handling

Explore how a small volunteer or business team receives, assigns and resolves non-sensitive requests. The organization needs clear ownership and recoverable handoffs.

People: A request submitter, coordinator and person doing the work; roles may overlap in a small team.

Access: Work with a consenting team and anonymized descriptions of past requests. Do not access its live internal system or confidential records.

Constraints: Use synthetic request content. Include reassignment, duplicate requests and differing permissions. Evaluate AI assistance as an option, including a reasoned decision not to use it.

- Where does responsibility become unclear?
- What information should each role see or change?
- How is an incorrect assignment noticed and repaired?

### Human-reviewed AI assistance

Explore a tool that drafts responses to non-sensitive community or product questions while a person checks and approves them. Value depends on reducing effort without losing control or accuracy.

People: People drafting responses and people responsible for their quality.

Access: Recruit adults who already answer repetitive questions. Collect anonymized workflow accounts, not private correspondence.

Constraints: Prototype static response scenarios; no model API or automated sending. Include unsupported claims, uncertainty, correction, rejection and a manual route. All generated examples are simulations.

- What must a reviewer verify before approving?
- When does reviewing take longer than drafting manually?
- How should uncertainty and source gaps be shown?

### Milestone fragments

- m16: choose the brief, investigate the existing workflow and define failure/evaluation scenarios. Both choices explore whether AI is appropriate.
- m17: map roles, access, handoffs and service dependencies; preserve competing design options.
- m18: independently prototype, evaluate and revise a bounded workflow; assemble evidence, limitations and the third draft case study.

## Case-study sections

- Context, users and business need

- Sources, competitive alternatives and synthesis
- Problem and evidence that support it
- Journey, structure and competing flows
- Wireframes, UI, system and prototype decisions
- Testing, accessibility and revisions
- Outcomes actually measured, or a proposed measurement plan
- Trade-offs, reflection and next questions
