Fictional example. The library, every person, every number and every file named here are invented for teaching. The margin notes explain why each part is credible; one claim is deliberately weak, and its note shows the repair.

Self-directed course project · four weeks of evenings · I did the research, design and testing alone

Members kept arriving for rooms that were not theirs: fixing the library’s booking confirmation

Promise: this case study shows a finding from three members becoming one wording decision, and what happened when the new wording was tested with people who had not seen it.

1. Context, users and business need

A small community library lends two meeting rooms. Members book by email and receive a short confirmation. The librarian wants fewer clashes at the door without adding software she must maintain.

Why this is credibleTwo sentences of background, then the need in the organisation’s own terms. Nothing here is a claim that needs evidence beyond the brief.

2. Research question, access, consent and limitations

Question: what makes members turn up for the wrong room or the wrong time? I interviewed the librarian and three members she introduced, after explaining that notes would be used for this project and could appear, without names, in a portfolio. Artefact: access-record.md · Tier: Real participant research

Nobody who had stopped booking was reached, because recruitment went through the librarian.

Why this is credibleIt says who was reached, how, what they agreed to, and who is missing. The exclusion bounds every later claim about “members”.

3. Sources, competitive alternatives and synthesis

Three of three members had once arrived at the right time in the wrong room. The librarian believed the problem was members not reading emails; all three members described confirmations that named the room by a number they did not recognise. Artefact: synthesis-page.md · Tier: Real participant research

Why this is credibleCounts travel with the finding, and the contradiction between the librarian and the members is kept rather than averaged into one theme.

4. Problem and evidence

The confirmation names rooms by number (“Room 2”), while the building’s signs name them by colour. Chosen because three of three members raised it, and it explains the clashes the librarian sees. Artefact: framing.md, read back to the librarian · Tier: Real participant research

Why this is credibleOne problem, with the reason it was chosen and a read-back that the person could have disagreed with.

5. Journey, structure and competing flows

Two flows were compared: sending a map with every confirmation, or naming the room by the colour on its door. The map was rejected because it doubled the librarian’s work for each booking.

Why this is credibleA rejected alternative with a cost-based reason shows a decision, not just an output.

6. Wireframes, UI, system and prototype decisions

The decision in depth: lead with the colour and the time window (“Green Room, 6–8 pm”) and put the number second. It costs a slightly longer subject line, and it matches what members see at the door.

Why this is credibleOne decision with its trade-off named. The other choices would get a line each rather than a paragraph each.

7. Testing, accessibility and revisions

Prediction written first: nobody would name the wrong room. Two people who had never used the library read the old and new confirmations on a phone. With the old one, both named the wrong room; with the new one, one of two still did, because “Green” appeared twice in the email. Artefact: test-record.md · Tier: Real participant research

Checked: the email reads in order with images off, and the colour is always written as a word. Not checked: a screen reader.

Why this is credibleA prediction made in advance, a count, and a partial failure reported plainly. Checks run and not run are separated.

8. Outcomes actually measured, or a proposed measurement plan

Members found the new confirmation much clearer, and clashes dropped.

Repaired: Not yet in use; no outcome measured. Plan: the librarian tallies door clashes for four opening days before the new wording and four after, with the same tally sheet. Artefact: measurement-plan.md · Tier: Proposed, not measured

The deliberately weak claim“Much clearer” rests on two testers, and “clashes dropped” on nothing at all: the wording was never used for real. The repair says what happened, what did not, and how it would be measured.

9. Trade-offs, reflection and next questions

Next: remove the second “Green” from the email and re-test with two new people; reach one member who stopped booking.

Why this is credibleEach limit has a small, specific next step rather than “more research would help”.
