Made-up example. Choosing portfolio claims, and choosing qualities.

**What I wrote:** User-centred, detail-oriented, collaborative. Three claims, and three sentences anybody could write about themselves.

**What a reviewer does with them:** Nothing. There is no artefact that could show detail-oriented, and no version of the portfolio that could fail to support it.

**The test I applied:** What would I point at? For user-centred, everything and nothing. For detail-oriented, nothing at all.

**What made those different:** Each names a file: the synthesis and framing page, the repair-and-re-test record, the limitations page. A reviewer can check all three in four minutes.

**Wrong turn:** The wrong turn is choosing qualities rather than claims, because qualities are what job descriptions ask for and they are easy to write. Nothing could support them and nothing could contradict them, so they do no work at all.

**Trade-off:** Specific claims are narrower and they exclude things you are also good at. Three narrow checkable ones beat six broad unprovable ones.

**Unknown:** Still unknown: whether these are the three a particular employer cares about. They are the three my evidence supports, which is the only set I can choose honestly.

### See the decision being made

Section: practice. Stable action: step-5-demo.

Made-up example. Checking whether three claims survive a skim, and checking by reading the portfolio properly.

**What I did:** Read all three case studies through, start to finish, asking whether each claim was there. It took about twenty minutes and every claim was there.

**What the reviewer does:** Ninety seconds. Headings, first lines, the images, and whatever the eye catches on the way down.

**What the skim actually reached:** Test-and-repair, from the before-and-after pairs, which are images and therefore unmissable. Stating limits, from a heading that says exactly that.

**What it did not reach:** Research-to-decision. The synthesis is in the middle of each page under a heading saying What we found, and the decision is three screens further down.

**What changed:** A first line under each case-study heading naming the decision the research produced. Nothing was added to the argument; the argument moved to where a skim meets it.

**Wrong turn:** The wrong turn is checking by reading, because reading is what you do with your own work and it finds everything. A claim that is present but not reachable in ninety seconds fails the only test that matters at this stage.

**Trade-off:** Putting decisions in the first lines means the pages open with conclusions rather than with the story, which reads less naturally to anybody who does read properly.

**Unknown:** Still unknown: whether ninety seconds is right. It is the figure people quote and nobody times themselves, so the check is a rough one deliberately.

<summary>Reading, video and deeper explanation</summary>

- The reviewer's question is narrow: could this person be handed a real problem and make it better without constant supervision? Everything in the portfolio either helps answer that or takes up space. Knowing the question is what lets you cut.
- The first pass is a skim of a minute or two, and it decides whether there is a second pass. That means the top of each case study has to carry the problem, your role and the outcome, because that is all a skim will reach.
- Writing your three claims first inverts the usual order and prevents the common failure, which is assembling everything you have and hoping a story emerges. Three claims — for example that you can turn research into a decision, that you repair work when testing shows it failing, and that you state limits — give every later choice a test.
- Craft still matters, but craft alone reads as decoration in this discipline, because a screen cannot show whether the problem was worth solving. Your visual background is an advantage only when it sits alongside the reasoning.

<summary>Reading, video and deeper explanation</summary>

- The promise check is mechanical: read the promise, read the case study, and decide whether a stranger would agree it was kept. A promise that is not kept means either the case study changes or the promise does, and the second option is usually right.
- Claims travel between pages. A number softened in one case study can appear inflated on the index page, and an about page written last is where unsupported summaries collect. Sweep the whole thing at once, including navigation, headings and the about page.
- Hosting is a choice with the same properties as a resource: it has a cost, an account requirement and terms. Whatever you choose, check it against this course's rules — free without a card, no trial that expires into a charge — and record what you verified and when, because the answer changes over time.
- A cold reader is the last useful instrument. Ask someone who does not know the projects to read it and tell you what you can do; the gap between their answer and your three claims is the portfolio's actual message, and it is usually not the one you intended.
