## Learning and clarity evidence

### Worked examples, connections and recall

The [IES practice guide, Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1), released in 2007, recommends combining worked solutions with problem solving, connecting concrete and abstract representations, using verbal explanations with graphics, revisiting content and asking explanatory questions. Its recommendations have different evidence ratings; do not present every recommendation as equally established.

Application proposed here: demonstrate a decision, let the learner complete one part, then ask them to use it in a new situation and explain why. Revisit an earlier concept when it becomes useful in a later module rather than imposing a daily review schedule. The guide addresses broader content-learning contexts, not a trial of this adult product-design course. It supports the direction, not a guarantee of improved outcomes or a fixed optimal word count.

### Understandable language is part of access

[W3C’s cognitive accessibility objective on clear content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/) addresses simple wording, short blocks, explicit meaning, images and alternatives to relying only on numerical understanding. It is supplemental guidance, not an additional WCAG conformance requirement.

Application proposed here: explain terms before asking for them, retain the actual label of a tool control, show one example beside the answer and teach quantities with a concrete example. Plain wording does not mean removing technical meaning. Validate comprehension with a learner’s explanation/application, not a readability score alone.

### AI: help during practice is not proof of independent learning

The indexed primary-paper abstract for [Bastani and colleagues, Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) describes a randomised high-school mathematics study comparing a general AI interface, a tutor with teacher-informed guardrails, and no AI. Its independent-performance finding motivates caution about answer-giving as teaching. This research is in mathematics, not adult design education; it does not validate HaruCourse prompts.

Verification limit: the publisher full-text retrieval returned 403 and the [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/) returned a browser challenge. Only indexed abstract-level information was inspected. No effect-size or full-method claim is made here. The proposed one-question conversations, attempt/hint/repair cycle and no-AI transfer check also follow the course’s existing independence requirement; prompt instructions cannot guarantee model compliance.

## Research decisions and remaining checks

1. Adopt in the plan: point-of-use explanation, concrete examples, supported practice, visible context and distinct progress states. These are proposed design decisions informed by the sources above.
2. Prototype optional AI activities as bounded teaching conversations, not answer generators or a source of guaranteed current facts. Keep the local written path sufficient.
3. Before assigning an external course segment: verify the exact relevant content, language, access and free alternative; record it in the canonical catalog. A homepage or course outline is not a verified assignment.
4. Before assigning a tool tutorial: execute the account workflow through save/export/reopen; check current labels and platform. Before assigning a video: verify playback or publisher subtitles and exact segment. No timestamps were invented in this research pass.
5. Test the actual AI prompts with free text access in at least two independently provided apps when available; record app/date, observed behaviour, limits and failures without requiring a named model. No such trials happened in this planning pass.
6. Observe Haru’s explanation, first action, return from AI and later unassisted transfer. This resolves the key unknown: whether the proposed experience addresses the reported confusion.

Link retrieval failures are not proof a resource is permanently unavailable. No research benchmark above has been assigned as required course work, and no new R ID has been created.
