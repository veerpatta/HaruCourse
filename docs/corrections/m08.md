## 2. Arithmetic and fact checks

- **Old example (wrong):** "a truncated axis that made nine look nearly twice fifteen". There is no fifteen; the counts are 9 unsure and 13 sure (9 + 13 = 22 ✓).
- **Old demo (wrong):** axis starting at 8 made "nine look like roughly a third of thirteen". Visible lengths are 9 − 8 = 1 and 13 − 8 = 5, so nine looks like **1/5** of thirteen; the true ratio is 9 ÷ 13 = 0.692 ≈ seven-tenths. Corrected in example and demo; the reader's reaction ("hardly anyone was confused") is consistent with 1 vs 5.
- **New check 1:** 45 and 55 from zero: 55 ÷ 45 = 1.22; from 40: (55 − 40) ÷ (45 − 40) = 15 ÷ 5 = 3.
- **Check 2:** 9 ÷ 22 = 0.409 → 41% (arithmetic right). One answer = 100 ÷ 22 = 4.55 points; 8/22 = 36.4%, 10/22 = 45.5%.
- **Supported:** 13 of 22 = 59%, so "most" is not false; feedback now says the problem is dropping the counts and sample.
- **Explanation alt text:** Saturday 34 + Sunday 21 = 55; 96 − 55 = 41 over five weekdays (mean 8.2), compatible with "fewer than 10 each" ✓ (kept).
- **Step 2 demo:** 9, 4, 10, 7 of 11 people, all ≤ 11; fewest = task two ✓.
- **Transfer (choir):** 12 ÷ 18 = 66.7% → 67%; from axis 5: (12 − 5) ÷ (6 − 5) = 7 vs true 12 ÷ 6 = 2; one member = 100 ÷ 18 = 5.6 points.
- **R63 verified 4 Oct 2026:** "Do not break the numerical axis on bar charts"; breaking a line chart's y-axis is acceptable when necessary and must be clear; avoid legends unless necessary; generally at most ten light grey gridlines; alt text via a table or description. Sample size is the course's own rule (R63 is silent), and is now attributed as such.
- **l11 transfer:** 3.9:1 < 4.5:1 required for normal-size text (WCAG 1.4.3) ✓.
